Human Exposure to Chemicals under Intermittent Emission and Contact

This tool converts steady-state estimates of chemical intake rates and biological concentrations into the time-averaged and peak values expected under intermittent chemical emission and/or human contact, without running a dynamic model.

Li et al. (2027) Modeling human exposure to chemicals from intermittent applications of consumer products using a steady-state modeling framework. J. Expo. Sci. Environ. Epidemiol.

Symbols and notes
SPulse emission rate during the application/emission period (mass/time). For example, if a cigarette containing 5 µg of nicotine is smoked over 10 minutes, the pulse emission rate is 0.5 µg/min. Calculate this rate using only the duration of active application or emission, excluding any intervals between episodes.
tEApplication/emission period (time).
tNPost-application/emission period, when release is negligible (time)
kE and RERate constant (kE) describing dissipation from the exposure-relevant compartment (1/time) and its corresponding residence time (RE in time). kE = 1 ÷ RE. Note that these parameters describe the overall dissipation rate from the exposure-relevant compartment (e.g., skin for dermal absorption or breathing-zone air for inhalation). Overall dissipation includes all processes that remove the chemical from that compartment, not just chemical reaction.
kB and t½Rate constant (kB in 1/time) and half-life (t½ in time) of the total (terminal) elimination from the human body. kB = ln2 ÷ t½. Note that these parameters describe the total elimination rate from the human body. Total elimination includes all processes that remove the chemical from the human body (e.g., biotransformation, renal clearance, exhalation, fecal egestion, etc.), not just biotransformation.
PBRPeak-to-baseline ratio of the intake rate
PBR0Your selected acceptable peak-to-baseline ratio used as a decision threshold
FScaling factor applied to ISS and CB,SS to get time averages
δUnitless timing term used to calculate scaling factors in the intermittent contact scenario. For details, see Eq. 8b

Enter tE, tN, kE and kB in any units, and this tool converts internally.
